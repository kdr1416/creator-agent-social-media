# Motion and visual QA

## Motion intent

Design each scene around a meaningful transformation, demonstration, typographic rhythm or data relationship. Supporting entrances/exits should clarify that idea. Choose timing, easing, anticipation, overshoot, holds and transitions per scene and approved style. Linear movement, stillness and minimal layers can be intentional. No universal easing, motion arc or rig is required.

Reuse components for continuity while varying action when meaning changes. Do not reject deliberate recurring motifs merely for being repeated.

## Review gates

- Run the selected engine's technical checks; clear errors before trusting layout/runtime results.
- Inspect opening, transformation-in-progress and final frames, plus playback. Record proof times; acknowledge when playback was unavailable.
- Compare against the approved brief/reference, including hierarchy, glyphs, content accuracy, target-size text, overlap, resolved assets, continuity and motion intent.
- Check requested subtitles/platform overlays only when enabled, using project-defined placement. No fixed footer exclusion applies.
- For generated footage, reject unusable text artifacts and accidental occlusion. Follow the light review / bounded regeneration contract before upscale.
- Use character performance checks only when characters are present; do not enforce a film-wide quota.
- Verify frame counts, dimensions, FPS, duration and master audio alignment with project tolerances. Quantization is cumulative at configured FPS, not per-scene duration rounding.

Technical success does not imply visual quality or human approval. Record measured results, remaining issues and explicit approval evidence in project state.
