# Visual and text design

Read the project brief and approved `creative/style-tokens.json` first. This reference supplies choices, not a universal art direction.

## Design system

Choose palette, fonts with required glyphs, spacing, format and hierarchy for the audience and brand. Store them once in project tokens. There is no default brand palette, font, padding or subtitle zone. Subtitles are optional; only reserve their measured area when enabled.

## Composition

Select character acting, product demonstration, typography, diagram, infographic, abstract transformation or mixed treatment from the message. Use balanced full-canvas composition and intentional negative space. Cards, screens, containers and open-canvas grouping are all valid when they support the approved style and hierarchy. The Boundary Removal Test can help evaluate an open-canvas treatment, but is not a universal rejection rule.

Layer assets only where independent animation, occlusion or depth needs them; no minimum layer count applies. Processes may use straight, curved or spatial paths. Characters/mascots are optional; when present, connect their role to the narrative rather than filling a quota.

## Text and value

Set a scene-specific visible text inventory and budget. Avoid accidental duplication of voice or labels unless the selected treatment needs it. Kinetic typography and data-heavy graphics are legitimate. Test essential text at the actual delivery viewing size; shorten or restructure unreadable copy. Use thumbnail/grayscale proofs when useful for hierarchy and contrast. Accent color and recurrence follow the project's design system, with no fixed color or interval.

## Footage and exact graphics

Only when generated footage is needed, follow Flow reference/batch/review contracts and avoid relying on generated typography. Composite exact text/data and inspect the plate before timing overlays. Reserve overlay space appropriate to the scene; avoid accidental face/product occlusion. Floating callouts and boxed/UI treatments are choices governed by the approved brief, not engine-wide laws.
