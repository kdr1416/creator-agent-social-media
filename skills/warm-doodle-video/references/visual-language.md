# Playful cartoon and mixed-media collage

## Colour relationships

Use light cream, peach or pale lilac grounds, with vivid orange/yellow, blue, red and green focal accents. A contextual scene may use a dark blue night setting. Avoid a uniform sepia cast, all-brown outlines or an all-muted sage/clay palette. Background softness and accent saturation are separate decisions.

Hex values in `assets/style-tokens.json` are proposed candidates, not sampled brand colours. The reference is compressed 360p; do not claim exact source values. Compare saturation, value and focal contrast side by side. Prefer dark neutral outlines and soft shadows. Paper grain is optional and must not define the whole look.

## Typography as animation

Use compact, friendly, readable sans text with moderate weight for ordinary copy. Short emphasis can use white lettering on a slightly tilted blue, red or green strip, with adequate contrast. A problem, feature and result can share consistent red/blue/green roles when supported by the script. This intentional headline treatment does not justify large generic information cards.

Let the strip arrive, reveal its short phrase in meaningful chunks, then hold. Keep Vietnamese glyph coverage and few text levels. Do not default to large dark bold left-aligned headings in every scene. Handwriting may suit a small annotation, but is not required everywhere. The exact reference font is unidentified. Segoe UI Bold and Latin-only Caveat are not approved substitutes.

## Asset language and mascot

Mix compatible sources deliberately: simple flat cartoon environments, expressive compact mascots, physical props, supplied screenshots/documents, tape, hand-drawn circles/checks/arrows and selective stickers. Coordinate line weight, shadow direction and saturation; mixed media does not mean unrelated stock styles.

A supplied screenshot can become the large central object, with tape and animated annotation. Crop or zoom to its meaningful detail instead of shrinking a whole page. Preserve provenance. Do not put tape on generic panels and call it collage. Never invent institutional documents, signatures, rankings or product UI as genuine evidence.

Prefer a compact original mascot with a clear silhouette, few facial marks, thin limbs and readable expressions at small scale. The reference's orange bean-like character demonstrates simplicity and energy, not a design to reproduce. A detailed human schoolchild is not the default substitute.

Physical action uses reach -> contact -> consequence -> reaction, with a maintained grip. A supporting mascot can point, track progress, inspect, celebrate or react to a specific event. Avoid both idle mannequins and forcing the mascot to manipulate every concept.

## Composition

Alternate meaningful visual families: cartoon situations, large central evidence/props and open mechanisms. A compact heading above a large changing object, with mascot/action in the lower safe area, is an available composition rather than a fixed three-row template.

Use large objects and close-to-wide reframing when it helps comprehension. Avoid repeated top-left titles over small isolated icons or an unexplained empty lower half. Respect the project's subtitle footer without over-reserving space. A feature counter can be a small attached label when progression matters, never a naked numbered node replacing the visual.

## Motion rhythm

Plan time-coded changes in attention, not just a list of tweens. The reference shows close-to-wide context reveals; colour strips with successive phrase reveals; documents followed by circles, ticks or detail crops; rotating mechanisms with character reactions; and occasional directional line wipes.

Use selective anticipation, pop/overshoot, phrase reveal, object travel, draw-on marks and small reactions. Preserve reading holds. A bounce belongs in this vocabulary when motivated; continuous bounce that substitutes for the main action is weak. Soft fades and small rotating icons alone reproduce the rejected pilot's quieter infographic rhythm.

Timing ranges in tokens are candidate defaults, not measured reference timings or rules for every scene. Align to actual spoken beats. Do not add an effect, transition or camera move to every beat. Audio/SFX character was not assessed; do not claim an audio match without listening.

## Comparison gate

Review colour, text, assets, composition, mascot and motion rhythm separately. For each, cite a reference frame/time and candidate evidence, then mark match/partial/mismatch. A technically valid render can fail all style axes. Future research may test frames, style boards or motion fragments when requested; a skill-only edit stops at the skill.
