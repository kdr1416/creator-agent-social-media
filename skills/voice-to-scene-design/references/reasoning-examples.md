# Reasoning examples — adapt, do not copy

These are invented teaching cases, not researched claims, required scene patterns or production-ready assets. Each shows why a design might fit, and what could make another treatment better. Do not reuse the setting or metaphor merely because a sentence contains similar words.

## A collection of fields is not a workflow

**Voice:** “Chương trình có Kinh doanh, Logistics, Kỹ thuật và Truyền thông.”

**Meaning:** areas within a programme; surrounding narration may add emphasis. It does not say that one area produces the next.

**Weak interpretation:** briefcase → parcel → gear → microphone, with a parcel running through them as a production line. Motion is present, but the invented causal relation becomes the explanation.

**One plausible design:** a student surveys a shared studio/workshop with a few recognisable activities. Attention moves between a planning surface, a delivery task, a working mechanism and a recording action, preserving one environment and visual hierarchy. Objects belong to activities rather than floating as equal icons. The character may explore or react without touching every object.

**Another plausible design:** a real supplied course page becomes the main evidence, with selective crops and a brief graphic annotation of the relevant fields. This may be clearer if the film already uses documents; it depends on that asset actually existing and being readable.

**Timing/style judgment:** in a very short slice, hold one composed visual and reveal attention selectively rather than showing four complete mini-scenes. In a spacious editorial film, use a different pace. A cartoon-collage treatment may mix props and actual media; a photographic style may show a shared studio. No subject noun mandates an icon or a path.

## A result can reveal the effort behind it

**Voice:** “Đằng sau ba thư mời là quá trình chuẩn bị nghiêm túc và định hướng rõ ràng.”

**Meaning:** reveal preparation behind an outcome; not necessarily three sequential preparation steps.

**Possible staging:** begin with the result, then shift attention to the work behind it—a marked draft, a consultation or a revisited choice—before resolving back to the result. The viewer's understanding changes even if the camera remains restrained. If the previous line already showed the offers, continuing that view may be more coherent than introducing a new desk scene.

**Alternative:** sustain the preparation activity and let the result enter later when the voice/context supports that emphasis. An invented diagram of “effort → three guaranteed offers” would overclaim causality. Illustrative envelopes should not impersonate genuine evidence.

**Design judgment:** choose which element owns the frame first, then add supporting props. Do not automatically include a checklist, compass, path, pen, folders, three labels and a celebrating character in a four-second scene.

## A problem sentence can be played as a situation

**Voice:** “Muốn tự học nhưng không biết bắt đầu từ đâu.”

**Meaning:** uncertainty and a need for direction.

**Possible staging:** someone repeatedly shifts attention between conflicting starting points while one unfinished task stays prominent. The scene resolves only if this beat or the next actually introduces a solution. Let acting, framing or selective detail express uncertainty; avoid pasting every spoken word around the character.

**Other possibilities:** a tangled route could work in a spatially driven style; a crowded document or device view could work when actual material is supplied. A literal maze is not compulsory. A worried mascot in a corner without a visible source of uncertainty is not a complete design.

**Continuity judgment:** when the next beat provides a plan, transform or simplify the same situation if useful. Do not change to an unrelated setting solely to create variety.

## Cause and effect must preserve the mechanism

**Voice:** “Khi một khâu bị chậm, những khâu sau cũng phải chờ.”

**Meaning:** propagation of a delay. A sequential mechanism may now be appropriate.

**Possible staging:** a paused handoff causes the following queue to compress; the viewer sees the delay travel through dependencies. Emphasise the blocked relation, rather than adding alarm icons everywhere. If the actual process includes parallel branches or buffers, a single chain could be misleading; check context before drawing it.

**Transfer judgment:** the parcel-chain idea that was weak for a collection of subjects can be useful here. The relationship, not the presence of the word “Logistics,” determines its suitability. Explain the mechanism using the chosen style's assets and rhythm, not a fixed universal conveyor animation.

## A comparison may need no character or animation flourish

**Voice:** “Hai phương án cùng mục tiêu, nhưng khác thời gian và mức hỗ trợ.”

**Meaning:** contrast along specified dimensions; no winner or superiority has been established.

**Possible staging:** a shared starting point and destination make the constant visible; differences in duration/support emerge through their respective journeys or supplied evidence. A different design may simply juxtapose two real objects with one meaningful distinction. No generic two-card layout is necessary, but follow the owning style/project's rules for actual surfaces and charts.

**Judgment:** do not force a mascot to touch both options, invent precise quantities, or add a dramatic winner reveal. A readable hold may serve the beat better than a complicated transformation. If values are absent, use qualitative distinctions honestly or flag the missing information.
